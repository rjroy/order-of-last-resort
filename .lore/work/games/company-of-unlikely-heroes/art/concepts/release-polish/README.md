## What to judge

- **Creation, commissions, recruitment:** Can you find the next action without reading every label? Does recruitment distinguish who is coming, who goes first, and whose loadout is open? The creation plate shows valid input, not a validation failure; the recruitment plate shows a full party.
- **Routes and battle:** Can you distinguish the immediate encounter from a hint about one possible later encounter? Once in battle, can you still read positions and threats without the book furniture competing with the board?
- **Forfeit, debrief, return:** Can you distinguish backing out from forfeiting, focusing a reward from claiming it, and winning the commission from retreating? The debrief’s lower strip and the two endings are alternate-state studies, not concurrent controls. The ordinary-debrief detail repeats the Knight’s HP gain from the claimed example; actual carry-over must come from the run.
- **Recovery and launch failure:** A guild that cannot be read is not an erased guild. Reload belongs on that page. A bad launch argument instead needs a legible diagnostic, not a button that cannot fix it.

The next useful in-game comparison, after a visual decision, is a full party at recruitment and a reward row with long descriptions at 1280 × 820. Those screens put the most pressure on the proposed typography, visible actions, and distinction between keyboard focus and selection.
