# Five hero pitches for the Company

Ronald proposed these kits on 2026-11-20. The pitch column records his ideas; the audit below is design feedback, not an approved revision. Bead: `mind-garage-djp`. No roster change is implied.

| Hero | Pitch: default movement, utility, offense |
|---|---|
| Rogue | **Retreat. Confusing Dodge:** swap places with the furthest character, friend or foe. **Wasn't Me:** can target an opponent only if they are next to someone else; redirect that opponent's threat to the other character. |
| Bard | **Hold. Inspiring Melody:** one random opponent bursts into laughter and skips a turn. **Healing Sonnet:** one random opponent screams in pain and takes damage. Progression should include **song weaving**; its rules are open. |
| Alchemist | **Pursue the closest ally. Healing Potion:** range 1, heal 1, plus a random side effect. **Acid:** range 3, deal 1 damage, plus a random side effect. This is a proposed kit for the Alchemist, not a description of the playable mix-in. |
| Blacksmith | **Pursue the closest opponent. Try This:** “reverse” all abilities. Examples: the Knight's push becomes a pull, guard becomes an area push, and the Wizard's blast heals instead. **Back Off:** deal 1 damage and push. |
| Necromancer | **Hold. Raise Dead:** requires a dead opponent; summon a zombie with half that opponent's health, maximum one zombie. **Summon Skeleton:** no prerequisite; summon a skeleton with 1 HP, maximum one skeleton. |

The zombie's utility, **Say Hello**, makes the closest character back away one space in fear. Its offense, **Offer Flower**, deals 1 damage at range 2: the flower is attached to a rock. The skeleton's offense, **Wave**, accidentally hits everyone nearby for 1. Its utility, **Don't Go**, prevents the closest character from moving.

Ronald's follow-up: both undead share a behavior profile, **“they just want to get to know you.”** They pursue opponents; their choice of ability can be random. A possible later Necromancer ability, **“Say hello to that one,”** would let the Necromancer direct the horde toward a particular opponent. This is a progression idea, not a starting command. Rounding half health for odd-health opponents is still open.

## Design audit: what changes when I bring this hero?

The [playable kits](../../../../../games/company-of-unlikely-heroes/README.md#the-company-of-unlikely-heroes) provide useful comparisons. The Healer draws attention **to herself**; the Rogue would assign blame **to a third character**. The Wizard chooses a target and suffers recoil; the Rogue trades both positions but cannot freely choose the partner. The current Alchemist has a nearest-target splash and a reflecting ward, neither of which is part of Ronald's proposed kit. The game forecasts the round before the player commits. For both musical targeting and potion reactions, decide whether the forecast shows the roll or the possibilities; reopening the preview should not silently buy a different roll.

**Rogue: I can put somebody else where the trouble is.** If the Knight is furthest away, a dodge can put the Knight in the Rogue's exposed square. If an opponent is furthest, the same order brings an enemy through the party line instead. Retreat changes who will be furthest next turn. That makes the habit part of the kit. Before prototyping, draw a case with two equally distant characters and one with two possible scapegoats. Decide who breaks each tie, whether a fellow enemy can take the blame, and whether redirection changes an announced attack or a later one.

**Bard: I choose what the song does, not who hears it.** With one enemy left, Inspiring Melody is guaranteed control. With three enemies left, it might interrupt the wrong one. Check the solo-enemy case first: can a standing order keep the last enemy laughing forever? Then sketch two rounds of song weaving where the first song changes the choice of the second. That would give the Bard a musical decision beyond choosing damage or denial. It is a test for the progression idea, not a proposed weaving rule.

**Alchemist: the dose is reliable; the reaction isn't.** Following a wounded ally keeps a range-one potion useful but can draw the Alchemist out of a safe firing position. The player can plan around exactly one point of healing or damage. The missing piece is an example side effect that would make them *choose a different patient, target, or moment*. Without one, the new kit risks playing like a healer with a ranged attack and a die roll. For the first paper test, write a small candidate effect pool and show what the player knows before committing. Unlike the Bard, the uncertainty is about the consequence, not the recipient.

**Blacksmith: I draft a different Knight by bringing the same Knight.** A pull instead of a push changes which side of a threat the Knight wants to stand on. Guard becoming an area push is not a mathematical inverse; it's an authored alternate ability. That distinction matters more than the damage on Back Off. Paper-test the Knight pairing first. Decide whose kit changes, for how long, and what happens to an already-issued order. Before promising roster-wide coverage, try to name a readable reverse for a summon or a song. If this moves forward, prefer explicit alternate abilities over code that tries to infer an opposite.

**Necromancer: an enemy defeat opens a new party decision.** Before the first kill, a skeleton can help fight but its Wave is dangerous beside allies. After a kill, the choice of corpse matters: a tougher opponent makes a sturdier zombie. Pursuing an opponent need not make the ability enemy-only: a zombie hurrying to meet an opponent could frighten a friend who happens to be closer when it acts. Sketch a crowded turn with both minions present: where do they appear, when do they act, and which greetings are legal from there? Open details include corpse consumption, behavior at the summon cap, Don't Go duration, and whether summons count for victory, wipe, or carry-over.

**Suggested first comparison:** sketch the same rescue turn twice, once with the Rogue and once with the current party. Keep the board and acting order fixed; mark the intended swap and the enemy's victim before resolving either turn. This cheaply tests whether the Rogue's apparent cowardice creates a rescue the player can see coming and wants to try. It does not require settling randomness, progression, or summon rules first.
