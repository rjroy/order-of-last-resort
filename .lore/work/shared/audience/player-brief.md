# Player brief for an audience of one

## Purpose

Explore whether AI can propose and refine games that Ronald enjoys and voluntarily returns to. Ronald is the initial sole intended player, not a proxy for a market. Learn from his play without treating one person's repeated sessions as independent player samples.

This brief captures his stated preferences and the synthesis agreed in conversation. It is a revisable starting point, not a validated model of his taste or a particular game's design.

## What draws him back

Ronald currently enjoys roguelite structures: a short-ish experience with a completion, followed by something that augments or changes the next play. His examples include *Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor*, *Vampire Survivors*, and *Net.Attack()*. He also enjoys *Path of Exile* 1 and 2, the *Borderlands* games, and the graphical logic puzzles of *Logic Town*.

His stated common thread is making choices and seeing how they change gameplay, puzzles, or both. Combat, randomized loot, a particular theme, or even a roguelite genre label is not established as mandatory by those examples.

## Agreed player promise

**Short, complete runs where choices reshape play, and completion opens enticing new ways to approach the next run.**

“Short” has no agreed minute count yet. The nature of completion, failure rewards, and permanent versus temporary progression remains open.

## Unlocks must earn their place

An unlock need not increase power. It must be “cooler”: a compelling new possibility whose anticipation and actual use justify unlocking it.

- It should change what the player can do, the decisions they make, or how they understand a challenge.
- Merely changing a class label, removing a chore, or offering an equivalent alternative is not enough on its own.
- **Anticipation:** Does understanding what it enables make Ronald want to try it?
- **Payoff:** Does using it create worthwhile new decisions or experiences?

These are questions for Ronald's reactions and play, not attributes AI can certify from a description. Do not confuse novelty, added complexity, or spectacular presentation with a demonstrated payoff.

The fighter/monk and ceiling-walking example in conversation illustrated this standard only. Ronald explicitly said not to implement it; it is not a concept seed or requested mechanic.

## Constraints and experimental boundaries

- Build playable experiments in Godot using the tools available here. Blender is available as an optional supporting tool; 3D is not required.
- Keep prototypes small enough to revise repeatedly. Verify relevant tool capabilities before implementation; availability alone does not validate an asset pipeline or export target.
- Preserve AI proposals and human interventions so AI-led design remains distinguishable from implementing Ronald's design.
- Keep enjoyment, desire for another run, and actual voluntary replay separate. Requested test runs are not voluntary replay.
- No requirement for commercial market testing, external recruitment, or an analytics service follows from this audience-of-one scope.

## Still to discover

The first hook and mechanic, run length, target platform/input, desired challenge and pacing, tolerance for randomness, and progression rules are open. Do not turn every open choice into a questionnaire before offering directions. Propose bounded hypotheses, ask what blocks useful comparison, and learn from tests.

## Repeatability

Keep this player brief separate from reusable procedure and experiment-specific conclusions. Record which revision of the brief each experiment used, along with any deliberate departures.

Replaying an experiment means restoring its builds, configuration, seeds, and starting progression. Repeating the method means starting a new experiment with an explicit input snapshot and a fresh record, without inheriting the previous game's conclusions. Neither restores Ronald's first-time experience after learning the game.
