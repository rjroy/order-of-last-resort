# Designing balanced commission finales

Working sketch for `olr-yfl`. Use this for the named destination at the end of
a commission. For optional encounters at a fork, use
[Designing balanced road missions](designing-road-missions.md).

The current finales need a balance pass. Treat them as cases to revisit, not
benchmarks to match. This sketch gives that pass questions to ask; it does not
approve new finales or prescribe changes to the existing ones.

## What is different at the destination?

The player chooses the destination when they accept the commission. Both roads
at the last fork lead there. The company arrives with the wounds, orders, and
upgrades it accumulated on the road. There is no next battle to save HP for:
the cost of taking a risk is whether the company finishes the job now.

The [commission tier schedule](designing-commission-tiers.md#slot-schedule)
places the short finale at T3 and the long finale at T6. For now, both draw
from the same unchanged destination pool. This does not establish that the
same finale is balanced for both arrival states.

## Starting rules

1. **Make the destination worth choosing.** Give the finale a named job and a
   tactical decision that pays off its premise. Write down the choice that
   should make the player reconsider their plan midway through. A finale can be
   more demanding than a road mission without just adding enemies, HP, or steps
   to the exit.
2. **Brief the job before the company sets out.** The commission board should
   tell players what they're trying to do and flag constraints that would
   change recruitment or preparation. On the battlefield, make progress,
   failure conditions, and the timing of decisive events visible. The player
   can discover a solution without having to discover what counts as a win.
   When the destination gets an illustrated report, use the
   [same child-scout sketch style](designing-road-missions.md#design-the-field-report-image-with-the-mission).
   Show one observation that makes the named job recognizable, not the solution.
   Hero or story requirements are a separate decision in `olr-e4h`, not an
   assumed exception to balance.
3. **Balance for arrival, not for a fresh standalone party.** Try the finale
   with different legal companies and plausible arrival states: wounded,
   lightly upgraded, and better prepared. Preparation should open better
   options, not substitute for an unannounced required hero or reward. A
   wounded company needn't always win, but it should have a meaningful decision
   to make rather than discover that the result was already settled on the road.
4. **Make urgency compete for the one direct command.** Moving one hero often
   means not commanding another hero to fight. Prefer pressure the company can
   interrupt, redirect, or race knowingly. A short timing window can create a
   good decision; a whole-mission countdown needs a stronger reason. If a
   deadline stays, count the commands needed to finish, show where the party
   can recover from a delay, and check that rushing past the fight isn't the
   only sensible answer. Reinforcements aren't a free replacement for a timer:
   check whether the company can keep the board playable as they arrive.
5. **Test the cheapest win and one disrupted plan.** A low-cost win is fine if
   it comes from reading and using the encounter, but not if the advertised
   threat can routinely be ignored. For **The Bridge Is Out**, start by
   comparing a straight run to the far bank with a plan that engages the
   enemies. Ask what makes either choice worthwhile and whether there is an
   answer after the first plan goes wrong. Treat possible changes, including
   interruptible bridge damage, as proposals for `olr-yfl`, not settled rules.

For the `olr-yfl` pass, record the intended decision for each current finale,
test at least one plausible arrival through an actual commission, and note
where play diverges from the intent. Use standalone to isolate rules, not to
declare a destination balanced. A scripted win and a player's judgment about
fairness answer different questions.
