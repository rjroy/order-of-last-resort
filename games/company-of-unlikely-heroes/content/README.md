# Company content catalog

[`catalog.json`](catalog.json) is the built-in manifest. It assembles [`common.json`](common.json) shared definitions, [`rewards.json`](rewards.json) outing rewards, the [`first_outing.json`](first_outing.json) and [`village_troubles.json`](village_troubles.json) adventures, the frontier files described below, and [`commissions.json`](commissions.json) commission data into one validated runtime catalog. A manifest contains only a nonempty `parts` array of paths relative to the manifest. Duplicate IDs in the same table across parts are errors, not overrides. For custom content, `--content=full-catalog.json` still accepts a complete catalog without a manifest. Each part and full catalog uses top-level dictionaries. Most are tables keyed by ID:

- **Adventures** list selectable character IDs in roster order, an ordered list of mission IDs, and a party size. The mission list determines the adventure's length.
- **Missions** name an encounter and may specify a positive integer `tier` for authored challenge or complexity, independent of adventure length and position. The `escort` objective names a character *placement* as its `subject` and a board *location* as its `destination`. `leave_zone` names a `subject` placement and a board `zone`; the subject starts inside and must finish the round alive outside the zone. `defeat_all` requires no subject and defaults to the `enemies` team. The frontier objectives are listed below. `failures` supports `subject_dead` (for `escort`, `leave_zone`, or `wagon`), `deadline` (requires a positive encounter deadline), and `party_wiped`. Every mission referenced by an adventure must include `party_wiped`: a wipe takes precedence over victory, and surviving summons do not count. Standalone missions may omit it. Optional `default_party` is an ordered list of distinct commandable hero IDs, exactly one for each encounter slot. Direct `--mission` launch uses this list; the guild instead deploys the selected party. Author `text`, `victory_text`, `defeat_text`, and `victory_event` on the objective. For escort and leave-zone outcome events, `{subject}` is the lowercased display name and `{subject_label}` preserves its display case.
- **Encounters** specify a board, optional round deadline and ordered placements. An absent or zero deadline is untimed. Optional `party_slots` are ordered deployment coordinates, separate from NPC and object placements. A placement has a unique `id`, one `character` or `object` definition ID, and an `at` coordinate. An optional `hp` overrides starting HP from zero through that definition's maximum; use `0` for a starting corpse. An optional `seed` sets the opening random state (default `1`, allowed `1`–`2147483646`). Party order precedes placement order. Most nearest-target ties prefer the first eligible unit in deployment order, then objects in placement order.
- **Boards** specify dimensions, a `default_tile`, tile overrides and named locations. Optional `zones` maps each name to a nonempty list of distinct, passable cells; rules and presentation use the same named zone. Optional `shore_cells` lists eligible cells for the safe-shore tint: only the orthogonally connected group containing the objective destination is highlighted. Omit it for no tint. It does not change tile artwork or rules. Coordinates are zero-based pairs of whole numbers. **Tiles** define passability, label, artwork key and optional `collapse_to`.
- **Characters** inherit their field values from **classes**; an override replaces the entire scalar, array or map, including `abilities` and `habits`. Keep each habit's behavior and the autonomous `behavior` compatible with the effective kit. `default_order` must be one of that character's habits. **Objects** and classes/characters list **capabilities** by ID, separate from their team and artwork.
- **Orders** provide player-facing labels. A character's `habits` map each order ID to a **behavior** ID. Labels are not executable IDs.
- **Events** are templates used by the rules. Use the supplied template names and placeholders when editing their text. Facts such as who was damaged and their coordinates are also available as event data. **Presentation** maps artwork/overlay keys to labels and optional colors.
- **Upgrades** optionally define between-mission rewards. Each entry specifies a commandable, non-minion `hero_id` (character, not class), `rarity`, positive integer acquisition `cap`, `label`, `description`, supported `effect`, and positive integer `amount`. The hero's effective kit and any referenced definitions must support the effect. The three unlock effects also name their granted `ability`; House Lights names the granted `order` and its `behavior`. Leave those grants out of the hero's base kit. Omitting `upgrades` preserves reward-free play.
- **Reward weights** optionally set positive whole-number `reward_weights` for all four tiers (`common`, `uncommon`, `rare`, `legendary`). With no weights the defaults are `60/28/10/2` respectively. For each deployed hero, the outing draws a tier among those with an eligible entry, then draws uniformly among that hero's remaining entries in that tier. Caps govern eligibility, not draw weight. Only the chosen reward uses an acquisition.

## Guild petitions and equipped kits

[`guild_progression.json`](guild_progression.json) defines the permanent petitions for the built-in guild. `kits` names each recruit’s base `offense` and `utility` ability IDs; Move is separate. A petition grants either `recruit` (one hero ID) or `ability` (one recipient, slot and ability ID). Its `cost` is paid once by the guild, not once per deployment. `prerequisites` lists other petition IDs. A `teaser` without a grant can communicate a future dependency but cannot be purchased, even after its prerequisite is met.

Ownership is separate from what is equipped. Borrowed abilities retain their existing rules and do not bring the donor’s habits or rewards. A character’s standing order must be compatible with the equipped kit. `requires_abilities` on an outing upgrade filters out offers for abilities the deployed hero is not using. Practice and standalone runs do not pay or consume guild points.

## Authoring commission reports

[`commissions.json`](commissions.json) adds report text and route eligibility to existing missions without changing their encounters or objectives. Commission catalogs supply all three of these optional top-level dictionaries together:

| Field | What to author |
|---|---|
| `mission_kinds.<kind_id>` | A nonblank `label` and a `descriptors` array of onward clues. Describe the kind of challenge shared by missions of this type, without naming an exact mission or giving details that apply to only one. |
| `mission_reports.<mission_id>` | An entry keyed by an existing mission ID, with `kind` referencing `mission_kinds` and a `descriptors` array for the immediate field report. Give concrete information about this mission, such as an enemy interaction, an objective constraint, or a board hazard. |
| `commission_settings.roster_adventure` | The ID of an existing adventure that supplies the recruit roster and party size. Its ordered mission list remains the fixed outing, not the commission route. |

On a mission report, the presence of `destination_descriptors` is the destination flag; there is no separate boolean. Its strings describe the finale's job for the commission board. Give a destination report the regular `descriptors` as well. Each descriptor array must be nonempty and contain only nonblank strings; multiple strings are alternate versions of the same report or clue. For example, `formation` clues describe enemies that depend on one another, `shield_wall` reports name the Shieldbearers protecting the Archer, and `last_wagon` destination text explains the job of getting supplies to the village.

Reports with `destination_descriptors` are eligible only as finales. Reports without it are eligible as transit missions. A mission with no report stays outside the commission pools even if it belongs to a fixed outing. Every reported mission must support the selected roster: its encounter needs exactly `party_size` deployment slots, its placement IDs must not collide with recruit IDs, and its failures must include `party_wiped`. An authored `default_party` is not needed for commission deployment.

The stock board offers three distinct destinations at lengths **5/5/10**, counting the finale in each length. Each transit fork offers two distinct missions of different kinds, so the chosen onward clue matches exactly one option at the next fork. At the last fork both reports point to the named destination instead. The stock data has 11 transit missions across four kinds and nine eligible finales.

A custom pool needs at least three eligible destinations and at least two transit kinds with a reported mission each. It does not need nine unique transit missions for a ten-mission commission: selection prefers an unvisited mission of the required kind, but can reuse one when that kind's pool is exhausted. Omit all three commission dictionaries to keep a prior fixed catalog working as before. With commission data present, `--fixed-outings` still opens the fixed catalog, and direct `--mission` launches use the same requirements as before.

## Supported rule handlers

The catalog selects code handlers, not arbitrary scripts. New mechanics need new rule code.

| Ability handler | Accepted `targeting.kind` | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| `move` | `cell` | No parameters; if present, target `range` must be `1`. |
| `apply_status` | `none` | `status` |
| `push` | `entity` or `automatic` | `collision_damage` |
| `bonk`, `blast` | `entity` or `automatic` | `damage`, `status`, optional `source_suffix` |
| `heal` | `entity` or `automatic` | `amount`; optional `prefer_wounded_self` for automatic healing |
| `enemy_heal` | `automatic` | Positive `amount` and `charges` in `params`. Use `targeting.teams: ["enemies"]`, no objects, and optional `targeting.range`. Heals the nearest wounded living teammate other than the actor. A successful execution spends one charge per actor and ability for the battle. |
| `pull` | `entity` or `automatic` | `recoil_damage`, `status` |
| `hook` | `entity` or `automatic` | No parameters. Moves a nonadjacent target one tile toward the actor if that tile is vacant. Neither damages nor moves the actor; it does not trigger attack redirection. |
| `attack` | `entity` or `automatic` | `damage`, optional `source_suffix`, `status` and `surround_bonus` |
| `swap_furthest` | `automatic` | No parameters; swaps the actor with the furthest eligible other living character. |
| `swap_ally` | `automatic` | `character` (ally character ID) and positive `range` in `params`. Swaps with a matching living teammate if both can enter each other's cells, once per actor and ability per battle. |
| `misdirect` | `entity` | `status` referencing a status with the `misdirect` handler. |
| `summon` | `cell` or `entity` | `character` referencing a non-commandable character with the `minion` capability; lower `priority` acts first after its summoner. |
| `sweep` | `none` | `damage` and a required `targeting.range`; hurts all other damageable characters in range. |
| `stop_target` | `automatic` | `status` referencing a status with the `stop_move` handler. |
| `repel` | `automatic` | No parameters. The nearest other character takes a step away if the destination is open; select the dominant axis, with horizontal breaking ties. A blocked step does not choose another character or direction. |
| `perform` | `automatic` | Exactly one of `status`, positive `damage`, or `damage_ability` referencing another performance with its own damage. Also requires `listener_event`, an event template. Selects a living opponent using the encounter seed. |
| `healing_potion`, `plain_tonic` | `entity` | Positive `amount` and finite `range`; include self and friendly characters, including civilians and summons. Only `healing_potion` draws a reaction. |
| `acid` | `entity` | Positive `damage` and finite `range`; only living hostile characters. Deals direct damage and draws a reaction. |
| `repeat_batch` | `entity` | No targeting filters: uses the bottled recipe's current targeting and excludes the original recipient. Does not draw or refill the bottle. |
| `nominate` | `entity` | No parameters. The necromancer nominates a living opponent for their own undead to pursue. |

Entity selection filters by `range` (Manhattan distance), `teams`, `objects` (include objects), and `capabilities` (all listed IDs are required). Without a range limit any distance is allowed. Automatic targeting selects the nearest eligible entity with ties broken deterministically. An automatic heal with `prefer_wounded_self: true` includes its wounded actor when it passes the other target filters; otherwise it targets the nearest eligible other entity, even at full health. Handlers also enforce the physical capabilities they need: `mobile` for movement and pull recoil; `pushable`/`pullable` for displacement; `damageable`/`healable` for damage/healing. `blocks_movement` is independent of whether an entity can be damaged or moved. `commandable` identifies units exposed to player commands; `protectable`, `recoverable` and `tauntable` govern applicable status effects.

`hook` needs a minimum target distance of two. If `targeting.range` is supplied, it must be at least `2`; omitting it places no upper bound on distance. The hook chooses the dominant axis toward the actor, with horizontal breaking ties. An occupied landing prevents the hook rather than moving the target along a different axis. A hook emits a `pull` event with `from_position` and landing `position`, but no `recoil_position`. An `attack` with `surround_bonus: {"character": "<id>", "damage": 1}` adds that damage once if another living ally of the specified character is orthogonally adjacent to the target. Check adjacency after misdirection and before any aura redirects the damage.

`swap_furthest` is an exception to nearest automatic selection: it excludes objects and breaks equal Manhattan distances by lowest instance ID, regardless of unit order. `misdirect` selects an opposing character with at least one other living character orthogonally adjacent. The player selects that opponent, not the alternate victim. At its next attack, the marked character redirects to the adjacent living character with the lowest instance ID, excluding itself and the original attack target. This can be its ally. If no alternate remains, the original attack proceeds; either way the mark is consumed. An aura can subsequently redirect the attack. The mark event names a currently adjacent candidate, while the attack forecast and resolution identify the actual recipient.

`perform` is also an exception to nearest selection. The player cannot supply a target, and allied, neutral (`civilian`), defeated and object entities are excluded. The handler uses the usual `teams`, `capabilities` and optional range filters. A damage performance affects its listener directly, without attack redirection; a status performance selects only opponents that can receive that status. If none qualifies, the action still takes place and reports `performance_dry` without drawing from the seed. As with other `automatic` actions, a `use_ability` behavior can make it a standing order; the actor's direct command replaces it for the round.

Alchemy treats civilians and heroes as friendly to each other, and compares other character teams relative to the caster or status recipient. Its [reaction pool](../rules/alchemy_reactions.gd) is shared by the two recipes, which favor opposite kinds of outcomes. Each caster advances its own dose stream; neither changing recipients nor consuming other encounter randomness rerolls it. An authored encounter seed takes precedence. Otherwise, an outing derives the dose seed from its seed, mission ID, and position in the outing; standalone missions default to 1. Reaction markers are emitted even if a target falls before its status can apply. End-of-round reactions check eligibility on the board after all activations and before objective movement; eligible heals resolve before HP losses, which are not a hit.

With `summon`, **cell** targeting places the creature on a vacant adjacent tile at its authored maximum HP; **entity** targeting selects a defeated opponent that matches the filters and is on a vacant tile. This consumes that corpse and uses half of its maximum HP, rounded up, as both the current and maximum HP of the new creature. Summons and already-consumed bodies cannot be raised. Each summoner can have only one living summon of a given character ID. Summons are added to the board immediately and join initiative starting next round, immediately after their summoner, ordered by `priority` then instance ID. Profiles with the `minion` capability may not include abilities that use the `summon` handler.

| Behavior handler | Parameters and compatibility |
|---|---|
| `use_ability` | `ability` without an explicit target (`none` or `automatic`). |
| `hold` | No parameters. |
| `pursue` | `ability` with entity targeting that accepts `team`, plus `move_ability`. |
| `rescue` | `ability` with entity targeting and a `pull` handler. |
| `threat` | A damaging `ability` with entity targeting, `move_ability`, `pursue_range`, `fallback_teams`, and `taunt_status` with a `retarget` handler. |
| `escort` | `move_ability`. |
| `hook_and_seek` | An entity `ability` using `hook`, `team`, `move_ability`, and `taunt_status`. Honors active taunt first; otherwise hooks the nearest legal target or takes one step toward the nearest member of `team`. |
| `follow_ally` | `move_ability`; approaches the nearest other friendly character, stopping when adjacent. Requires the `follow_ally` event template. |
| `most_injured` | Healing `ability` with entity targeting; uses it on the legal recipient missing the most HP, or holds if everyone it can reach is healthy. |
| `nearest_target` | Entity `ability`; uses it on its nearest legal recipient or holds if none qualifies. |
| `follow_party` | `move_ability`. Takes one legal step along the shortest path to adjacency with the nearest living commandable hero in deployment order on ties. Excludes minions and civilians, and holds when already adjacent. The move event names that hero and the landing tile. |
| `retreat` | `move_ability` and opposing `team`. Step away from the nearest living member of that team, with lowest instance ID breaking enemy-distance ties. Among equally distant legal steps, prefer the direction most aligned with the vector away from that enemy, then the fixed direction order. Hold if no step increases distance. |
| `greet` | `move_ability`, opposing `team`, `greeting_range`, and a nonempty list of `greetings` ability IDs from the effective kit. With an opponent within `greeting_range`, picks among legal greetings. An entity greeting picks its nearest legal target; automatic greetings use their own targeting rules. Otherwise, takes one step along the shortest path to a reachable tile within `greeting_range` of the nearest opponent where at least one greeting is legal on the current board. It waits if no such path exists. |
| `retreat_attack` | `team`, damaging entity `ability`, and `move_ability`. Retreats if adjacent to an opponent and a legal move increases distance from the nearest threat. Cannot retreat again the next round; otherwise attacks or pursues. |
| `finite_healer` | `team: "enemies"`, `heal_ability` using `enemy_heal`, damaging entity `ability`, and `move_ability`. Heals when legal, otherwise attacks or pursues. |
| `swap_ally` | `team`, `ally`, `swap_ability` using `swap_ally` for that ally, damaging entity `ability`, and `move_ability`. Swaps if the actor is closer to the opposition than the ally, otherwise attacks or pursues. |

Every `move_ability` must refer to a `move` handler and belong to the effective kit. The supported status/expiry pairs are `reflect` / `actor_turn_start`, `skip_action` / `consume_action`, `retarget` or `stop_move` / `actor_turn_end`, and `misdirect` / `consume_attack`. These statuses belong to characters with turns. A crate can still take the damage from a status-inflicting attack, but does not acquire its turn-based status. `reflect` takes a `range` and can filter its redirect targets with `teams`, `objects` and `capabilities`; `retarget` can filter permitted sources with `teams`. `misdirect` takes no parameters and persists until the next attack. `stop_move` takes no parameters and blocks only the Move ability until the end of the affected character's next turn.

`skip_action` may specify a `skip_event` for an upright skip instead of using the default `recover`, which represents getting up from a knockdown. Both prevent the next action. The status may also give a positive `immunity_rounds` and an `immune_label`. That status cannot be refreshed while pending or immune. After consuming the skipped turn, the same status becomes an immunity marker for the rest of that round and the specified number of full following rounds. Other status IDs are unaffected. The Bard's `laughter` uses one full following round and a dedicated `laugh_skip` event; ordinary `knockdown` does not grant immunity.

Catalogs with a `swap_furthest` or `swap_ally` ability must provide the `swap` event template. A `misdirect` ability needs `misdirect_mark`; a `misdirect` status needs `misdirect_redirect` and `misdirect_missing`. `summon` needs `summon` for cell targeting or `raise` for entity targeting. `sweep`, `repel`, `stop_target` and `follow_party` need events of the same names. `hook` needs `hook_pull`, and a configured surround bonus needs `surround_bonus`. `perform` needs `performance_dry` and the configured `listener_event`. Listener events receive `{actor}`, `{ability}`, and `{target}`; damage performances also provide `{amount}`. Catalogs without these handlers need not include their templates. Use the placeholders in the built-in templates when authoring replacements.

## Authoring frontier missions

Use [`frontier_combats.json`](frontier_combats.json) for combat encounters and examples of enemy kits, [`frontier_objectives.json`](frontier_objectives.json) for objective-driven missions, and [`frontier_outings.json`](frontier_outings.json) for guild roster and mission order. Keep stable mission IDs for direct launches and replay fixtures. To support both guild and standalone play, give the encounter ordered `party_slots` and the mission a `default_party`; if there are slots but no default party, launch through the guild. Fixed fixtures without slots use their authored placements. Test a standalone launch with `godot --path games/company-of-unlikely-heroes -- --mission=<id>` from the repository root, plus a restart and a guild launch with a selected party.

The [objective schema](objectives.gd) distinguishes `board.locations` (one coordinate per ID) from `board.zones` (nonempty sets of distinct cells). Both use in-bounds, initially passable cells. Use names, not inline coordinates, in these objective fields. Subjects refer to character placements in the encounter, not character definitions. [Objective rules](../rules/frontier_objectives.gd) run round-end effects before checking victory. Party wipe and objective-specific losses such as excess noise take precedence over victory.

| Objective | Fields and round-end goal |
|---|---|
| `pressure_gate` | `plate`, `gate`, `exit` are locations; the plate and gate must be different cells. Any living character on the plate opens the gate immediately. A living deployed hero must finish at the exit. |
| `survive` | Positive `turns`. Keep a deployed hero alive through that round; killing all enemies does not end the mission early. |
| `herd_sheep` | `subjects` lists distinct sheep placements; `pen` is a zone with at least one cell per sheep. All sheep must finish alive in the pen after unpenned sheep flee nearby characters. |
| `collapsing_bridge` | `exit` is a location; `collapses` is a nonempty list of `{round, cells}` entries with positive rounds and `cells` naming a zone. Scheduled cells become impassable `void` and defeat occupants before checking for a living deployed hero at the exit. |
| `treasure_noise` | `treasure` is a location; `threshold` is a nonnegative noise budget. A living deployed hero must reach the treasure. Executed hero Moves cost 1 noise, performances cost 3, other abilities cost 2, and holding is silent. Exceeding the budget loses the mission. |
| `ritual` | `circles` is a zone; `required_turns` is positive. Every circle must hold a living character, including enemies or summons, at the end of that many consecutive rounds. An empty circle resets progress. |
| `wagon` | `subject` is the wagon placement; `destination` is a location. The wagon advances one legal step at round end unless an enemy is adjacent or its route is blocked. It must reach the destination alive. |

To stage an enemy wave for `survive`, author `reserve_round` and `reserve_placements` together. Use an integer from `2` through `turns` and a nonempty list of distinct enemy character placement IDs. Author those placements in the encounter with valid, distinct starting cells; they stay off the opening board. Clear entries join initiative for the scheduled round. Blocked entries wait and retry for later rounds through `turns`, without displacing occupants. See Hold the Line for an authored example.

**Combat traits.** `traits` is a profile array, replaced as a whole by a character override. `adjacent_guard` protects the named character ID; `adjacent_damage_bonus` buffs the named ally's attack. `death_blast` can chain through living characters on either team; `retaliate` needs a surviving defender and an attacker still in range. `ally_death_bonus` counts fallen teammates matching `allies` and scales exactly one of `damage`, `reduction`, or `movement`. Use `max_reduction` to cap that trait's armor (the authored Bandit cap is 1). Adjacent guard can absorb a hit completely; ally-death armor cannot reduce a remaining positive hit below 1 damage. Extra movement follows legal paths rather than jumping over occupied cells. See [combat traits](../rules/frontier_combat.gd) and the authored profiles for parameters.

**Make rules visible.** Give the objective short, actionable text and explain danger before it resolves. Use the [battle cues](../battle_cues.gd) and rule-derived progress and markers for board glyphs, legend, tile inspection, and forecast. Check that reused enemy art has distinct role labels and that inspection explains traits and special abilities. Check ordered forecast events for reactions, uses left, losses, and round-end changes; do not rely on color alone.

**Verify changes.** From the repository root, use `godot --headless --path games/company-of-unlikely-heroes --script res://tests/<suite>.gd`. Run `frontier_catalog_validation_test` for authored field checks, `special_objectives_test` and `combat_encounters_test` for rule interactions, and `objective_cues_scene_test` for standalone markers and forecast. The [`fixed_outing_victory_test` guide](../tests/fixed_outing_victory_test.md) covers checked-in routes replayed through the guild scene with real carryover and rewards. The optional [`fixed_outing_route_search`](../tools/fixed_outing_route_search.gd) tool finds candidates; validate them with the scene replay rather than treating a search result as proof.

## Loading and snapshots

`Catalog.read_default()` loads editable definitions; `Catalog.read_source(path)` runs the same JSON and schema checks for custom files and returns either `{definitions}` or `{error}`. `Catalog.validate(defs)` accepts JSON-shaped data, including whole Godot floats produced by the JSON parser, and returns path-qualified errors. `Catalog.build(defs, mission_id, party_ids = [])` fails with `{}` for invalid data, invalid picks or an unknown mission. A supplied party fills slots in order, using character IDs as unit IDs. An empty party is valid only for fixed encounters without slots; callers that want the authored demo party must pass `default_party` explicitly. A successful build keeps only definition IDs and encounter/runtime values on entities. Statuses are stored once in each entity's `statuses` dictionary.

The state holds a `catalog` reference with a detached, recursively read-only `defs` dictionary. Unit class and team and the encounter deadline come from those definitions, not mutable copies. The objective destination and `outcome` are the runtime authorities for the exit and result. Deep-copying state for forecasts shares the catalog reference while copying mutable units, terrain, orders, statuses and encounter random state. Greeting and performance choices advance the random state only in the forecast result; committing that forecast keeps the same choices. Rebuilding the same mission starts from the authored seed. Editing the original definitions or building a second mission does not modify an existing state.

`Adventure.start_adventure(defs, id, picks, reward_seed)` accepts an optional integer seed for repeatable rewards and unauthored dose streams; if omitted it chooses a fresh seed and stores it in `run.outing_seed`. Commissions default to the offer's seed for unauthored doses; supplying the optional fourth argument makes rewards and those doses repeatable from that argument without changing route draws. Reward draws use their own RNG, independent of forecasts and encounter seeds. Only committed non-final victories with eligible entries require a choice. The selected hero's counts deploy with that hero and reset when the company returns to the guild. Max-HP rewards raise current HP only by the new maximum gained since the last mission, preserving missing HP; debrief results still describe the completed mission.

With **Me or You**, a wounded Healer still takes the entire Heal. At full HP, the Healer instead divides that amount evenly among the other living deployed teammates (including teammates already at full HP, but not summons). Party order breaks ties for indivisible points. A teammate's unused share is lost rather than redistributed.

Village Troubles uses tier 1 for the Goblin Mob and tier 2 for the Harpoon Squad and Unwelcome Guest. These are authored complexity labels, not measured difficulty. The Guest's troll is a targetable opponent with a movement-only kit; carried damaging orders can injure or kill it even if it is pushed outside the square earlier in the same round.

To check the built-in Bard without a standalone mission, run `godot --headless --path games/company-of-unlikely-heroes --script res://tests/bard_test.gd` from the repository root. `adventure_scene_test.gd` also checks recruitment, deployment and both standing-order controls in the default adventure.

Godot reads the manifest and its parts directly with `FileAccess`; no import step is needed when running from source. For a Godot export preset, include `content/*.json` under **Filters to export non-resource files/folders**. The project does not ship a platform export preset.
